# Using TiffinDB

This guide is for the people who use TiffinDB in the browser: adding and changing records, finding them again, and keeping their files. The last chapter is for Admins, the people who also set up projects and accounts.

Installing TiffinDB on a server, upgrading it, and backing up its data folder are covered in the [host documentation](../host/README.md), for the person who looks after the server.

## Chapters

1. [First steps](first-steps.md): signing in, the project list, finding your way around a project, signing out.
2. [Records](records.md): adding, changing, and saving records; linking records; moving them to the trash.
3. [Lists and search](lists-and-search.md): finding, sorting, filtering, and searching every table.
4. [Collections](collections.md): saved searches that keep themselves up to date.
5. [Files](files.md): adding, opening, replacing, and removing files and pictures.
6. [The trash](trash.md): getting records back, and emptying the trash.
7. [Forms](forms.md): for Admins, choosing what the record screen shows.
8. [Import and export](import-and-export.md): bringing rows in from CSV and Excel files, taking records out, and project archives.
9. [Display settings](display-settings.md): colours, text size, lettering, and movement.
10. [For Admins](for-admins.md): people and sharing, tables and fields, plugins, snapshots, deleting a project, and the activity log.

## How this guide is written

- Button labels and screen names are written in bold, exactly as TiffinDB shows them, for example **Move to trash**.
- Examples use a made-up project called Home Inventory, with a table called Items and records such as Stand mixer.
- Some labels include the name of your table or record. Where this guide says **Add item**, your button says "Add" followed by whatever one record in your table is called.
- There are no screenshots. Your screen can look different from someone else's, because each person chooses their own colours and lettering (see [Display settings](display-settings.md)).
- Every screen works without JavaScript. Where JavaScript changes how something behaves, the chapter says so.
